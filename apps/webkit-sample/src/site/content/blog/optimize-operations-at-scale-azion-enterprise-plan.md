As an application grows, decisions are no longer limited to technical capacity. Usage volumes become more predictable, costs carry greater weight in planning, and the operation may require commercial terms and support levels tailored to the business.

This does not mean that every growing application needs to move to Enterprise. Pro can continue supporting commercial and production projects as usage increases. Enterprise starts to make sense when an operation can benefit from usage or spend commitments, reserved capacity, and customized terms.

---

## What You Get With Enterprise

Enterprise adapts Azion’s commercial and operational model to the needs of each business:

- **Cost optimization:** usage or spend commitments may provide discounts on on-demand pricing.
- **Capacity planning:** Reserved Capacity lets you secure the capacity your operation needs in advance.
- **Customized terms:** limits, usage requirements, and payment terms can be defined according to the company’s profile.
- **Governance for complex organizations:** a customized number of Workspaces and RBAC at both the team and workspace levels help manage access across multiple teams, brands, or business units.
- **Support for compliance requirements:** Azion’s certifications, reports, and security capabilities help support vendor assessment and approval processes in regulated industries.
- **Specialized support and services:** companies can purchase advanced support tiers and Professional Services based on their needs.
- **Continuity on the same platform:** applications remain on the Azion Platform, with no need to rebuild their architecture.

## Governance for Multiple Teams and Operations

When different teams, brands, or business units use the same infrastructure, managing permissions and separating responsibilities become part of the operation.

While Hobby and Pro offer a single Workspace, Enterprise lets companies define a customized number of Workspaces based on their organizational structure. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) can also be applied at both the team and workspace levels.

This helps companies with multiple engineering teams, parent companies, multi-brand groups, and other complex organizations manage environments and access with greater granularity.

## Compliance for Operations With Specific Requirements

In regulated industries, choosing an infrastructure provider also involves security, auditing, and compliance criteria.

Azion is certified as a **PCI DSS 4.0.1 Level 1 Service Provider** and maintains **SOC 2 Type 2 and SOC 3 reports**. For Enterprise customers, this evidence can support vendor assessment and approval processes. **FIPS 140-2 Level 3** is available through Secure Key Store, offered as a paid add-on.

These certifications and capabilities are relevant to financial services, healthcare, retail, and other regulated industries. However, adopting Enterprise does not automatically make an organization compliant: each company remains responsible for its own controls, configurations, and regulatory requirements.

## Support and Services for Critical Moments

In addition to a customized commercial model, Enterprise customers can purchase advanced support tiers and Professional Services for specific operational needs.

Available options include:

- **Business Events Support:** specialized assistance with preparing for and supporting high-impact events, such as campaigns, launches, or seasonal traffic periods.
- **Security Response Team:** access to specialists for threat analysis, attack investigation, and mitigation guidance within the contracted scope.
- **Technical Account Manager:** a technical point of contact who follows projects, reviews plans, and recommends architectural and operational improvements.
- **Managed Configuration Service:** ongoing specialist assistance with environment configuration and remediation.
- **Best Practices Review:** an assessment of architecture and configurations to identify opportunities for improvement.
- **Instructor-Led Training:** expert-led training tailored to the needs of each team.

The availability, scope, and terms of these services depend on the selected support tier and contracted services.

## When Enterprise Is Worth Considering

Enterprise may be a good fit when:

- usage has reached a significant and predictable volume;
- a usage or spend commitment can generate savings;
- the operation needs to reserve capacity in advance;
- multiple teams, brands, or business units need separate Workspaces and access controls;
- compliance requirements are part of the vendor approval process;
- critical events or sensitive operations require specialized support;
- customized limits and commercial terms make planning easier.

The decision should consider the concrete benefits the model can bring to the operation. More than application size, the assessment should account for usage predictability, organizational structure, compliance requirements, and the need for specialized support.

## What Stays the Same

Moving to Enterprise does not mean changing platforms. The products, capabilities, and tools used to build and operate the application remain within the Azion Platform.

This allows teams to retain their workloads, configurations, and processes while adjusting capacity, support, and commercial terms to the operation’s new requirements. A project can begin as an experiment on Hobby, move to commercial use on Pro, and adopt an Enterprise model when doing so becomes advantageous.

## How to Choose the Right Model

The choice between on-demand pricing, Reserved Capacity, and a Savings Plan depends on the operation’s usage patterns.

When usage still varies significantly or cannot be predicted reliably, the on-demand model may provide the necessary flexibility. If capacity requirements are known in advance, Reserved Capacity may offer terms better aligned with that volume. When total spending is predictable, a Savings Plan may provide more favorable terms.

The assessment should consider usage history, growth expectations, and the level of commitment the company can make. Azion’s team can help evaluate these factors and define a model aligned with the business.

## Quick Questions

**Does every large-scale application need to use Enterprise?** No. Pro can continue supporting applications as their usage grows. Enterprise should be considered when customized terms, commitments, reserved capacity, or advanced support provide clear benefits to the operation.

**What is the difference between Reserved Capacity and a Savings Plan?** Reserved Capacity is based on an upfront capacity commitment. A Savings Plan is based on a spend commitment over the contracted period.

**Does Enterprise have a fixed price?** No. The model is customized according to usage requirements, payment terms, and the commitments agreed upon with the company.

**Do I need to migrate my applications to another platform?** No. Enterprise is adopted within the Azion Platform, allowing teams to continue using their existing projects and resources.

**How do I know whether a commitment will be advantageous?** The decision depends on usage volume, predictability, and the type of commitment the company can make. Azion’s team can help assess the most suitable model.
